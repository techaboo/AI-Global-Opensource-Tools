# Governance

## Project model

The Open Source AI Atlas is owner-led. The repository owner holds final responsibility for project direction, access, releases, deployments, policy, and merges. Maintainers may be delegated review or operational responsibilities in the public [maintainer record](MAINTAINERS.md).

## Decision making

Routine catalog corrections and implementation changes are decided through pull-request review. Maintainers should seek evidence-based consensus, explain requested changes, and keep substantive decisions in public issues or pull requests.

The repository owner has final authority when consensus is not reached. Significant changes—including taxonomy redesign, inclusion-policy changes, data-source changes, privacy-affecting features, governance changes, or removal of established entries—should receive an issue discussion before implementation when practical. The final decision and rationale should be recorded publicly, except for confidential security, privacy, legal, or conduct matters.

## Roles

- **Repository owner:** controls repository access and has final decision and merge authority.
- **Maintainer:** a person publicly listed in `MAINTAINERS.md` and delegated review or operational authority.
- **Contributor:** anyone proposing code, data, documentation, design, or issue reports.
- **Community member:** anyone using or discussing the project under the Code of Conduct.

A GitHub permission alone should not be treated as a public maintainer appointment. Maintainer changes require an update to `MAINTAINERS.md` by the owner.

## Reviews and changes

At least one maintainer approval is expected before merge. Self-merge should be avoided for material governance, security, privacy, methodology, or accessibility changes when another qualified reviewer is available. Emergency security changes may be merged privately or quickly and documented after disclosure is safe.

Conflicts of interest should be disclosed. A reviewer closely affiliated with a cataloged project should avoid being the sole decision-maker for disputed inclusion, ranking, “Hot,” status, or removal decisions.

## Catalog stewardship

The scheduled repository-metric baseline runs weekly. Editorial catalog changes require review. Maintainers target a quarterly sample audit of links, licenses, descriptions, statuses, and “Hot” labels, subject to volunteer capacity. The methodology, not commercial relationships or contributor prominence, governs inclusion.

## Conduct and appeals

Conduct reports are handled under [CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md](CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md). A contributor may appeal a catalog or review decision by opening a focused issue with new evidence. The owner issues the final project-level decision and should document the rationale.

## Amendments

Governance amendments use the normal pull-request process and should be announced in an issue. Material changes require owner approval and an update date in the merged change history.
